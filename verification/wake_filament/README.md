# Structured Vortex-Ribbon Wake

2026-11-06. This experiment keeps the P1 Galerkin wall, Kutta solve and
P1 triangular wake influence in the wall matrix. It is selectable in Web
alongside the triangular-sheet update, while fixed wake remains the default.
It implements a VSPAERO-style material-line
direction update, not a transcription or equivalence claim for VSPAERO.

## Model

The solver accepts a structured wake generated by `make_wing` or reconstructed
from a restricted CAD wake: contact-free, x-extruded, level downstream
boundary, with a material Gamma basis. Unsupported inputs are rejected;
the remeshing checks are not a general certificate for arbitrary CAD topology.
Each material line starts at a declared TE attachment and the
P1 Gamma basis is constant along that line. A target is reconstructed from
each line root by aligning successive segments with local 3-D velocity and
preserving their initial segment lengths; the actual update is relaxed.
Gamma labels and connectivity remain attached to the moving material nodes.

For the *velocity used in this update*, the P1 wake is approximated with
midpoint-strength vortex ribbons. Each spanwise P1 interval can be split
into several ribbons. The P1 wall contribution is evaluated by centered
finite differences of its analytic potential; the wake ribbons use the
existing Rosenhead-Moore regularized segment kernel. Thus the wall solve
uses an unregularized P1 sheet, whereas kinematics uses a regularized,
piecewise-constant ribbon approximation. They are not the same operator.
Core radius and subdivision must be reported; no core is a calibrated
viscous model. The ribbon/P1 off-sheet consistency test does not establish
near-sheet convergence.

The update requires a locally nondegenerate triangle and guards against
an abrupt reversal relative to the previous step, not against rotations
greater than 90 degrees relative to the initial sheet. An optional
`--intersection-guard` backtracks when nonadjacent wake faces cross or
overlap. Its audit skips shared-node pairs, wall crossings and other groups;
it is not a complete geometry certificate. A solver status of `converged`
only refers to declared mapping, line-direction and face-slip thresholds,
and does not constitute physical validation.

## Velocity Timing

On the final 30x32, 10c snapshot, 512 identical face-center targets and
the same saved wall potential and Gamma, Windows/myml single-thread CPU:
after one warmup, three-repeat median `wake_velocity` took 4.216 s and the
existing P1 two-sided probe evaluator took 9.059 s. Their total velocity RMS
difference was 0.015061 in unit-freestream velocity units. These are
different regularizations and wake discretizations, so the approximate
2.15x timing ratio is not a same-model acceleration or an accuracy result.
It measures the velocity evaluation only, not wall assembly or whole solve.
The raw timings and command are in `filament_benchmark_full.json`.

## Same-State Cross Evaluation

`tools.wake_velocity_crosscheck` evaluates the two velocity models at the
same face centers and normals, on a single saved geometry and wall state.
For an older snapshot without wall potential it recomputes the Galerkin wall
and pressure Kutta solution on that fixed geometry. These are different
near-sheet operators, not two implementations of the same operator.

On the 30x32, 10c filament snapshot after 12 updates, sheet-probe slip RMS
was 0.011036; the ribbon result at core 0.05c / subdivision 2 was 0.010915,
but the RMS of the *facewise slip difference* was 0.010451. On the original
sheet-update snapshot after 10 updates, recomputing the wall gave sheet
slip 0.018976 and ribbon slip 0.019104, with facewise slip difference
0.004838. Similar aggregate RMS therefore does not imply equal local
velocities. The latter result is `cross_full_sheet.json`, and the former is
`cross_full_filament.json`; both scans also contain other cores/subdivisions.
They used overlapping processes, so their timings are not benchmarks.

On the 10x12 guarded filament snapshot, the default sheet probe produced
slip RMS 0.661996 and maximum 8.78756. The x, y and z finite-difference
segments for face 166 cross face 165; the stencil crosses another face in
five axis/side instances overall. At probe fractions 0.0275 and 0.01375,
the segment audit found no crossings and slip RMS was 0.024625 and
0.024576, respectively. A larger probe fraction 0.11 also crosses other
faces and gives RMS 0.325062. This is direct evidence that a finite
difference can sample a potential jump as a spurious velocity; the audit
tests the gradient segments only, not every possible branch or geometry
failure. At the time of that cross evaluation no formal solver behavior
had been changed; see the subsequent probe repair below.

At the same small snapshot, using probe 0.0275 as the sheet reference,
core 0.05c with 2/4/8 ribbons per interval gives ribbon slip RMS
0.019730/0.026338/0.025039 and facewise slip-difference RMS
0.010612/0.006366/0.005566. Two probe values and three ribbon resolutions
on one geometry are sensitivity checks, not a joint core/mesh convergence
or accuracy proof. Detailed hits and results are in
`cross_small_guard_audit.json` and `cross_small_guard_reference.json`.

## Route Decision

Keep the Galerkin wall and P1 triangular wake for the production fixed-wake
baseline and as the potential/strength representation. Neither current
free-wake update is ready to be the dependable default: the original can
block on initial orientation and its probe stencil can cross another face;
the ribbon prototype can fold or self-intersect, and its velocity operator
differs from the wall sheet. Continue the material-line/ribbon route as a
research candidate, not as a proven replacement for the triangle sheet.
Investigate local probe/stencil intersection detection and adaptive
one-sided distance with manufactured near-sheet tests, and test a
face-consistent material-line update with complete geometry guards. Compare
on common snapshots and common independent diagnostics before comparing
converged loads and full-solver time.

## Probe Crossing Repair

The P1 two-sided evaluator now checks each center-to-probe path and each
finite-difference segment against other wake and wall triangles. It halves
that face's probe height and difference step together until no transverse
crossing is found, and reports adjusted face count and maximum halvings.
If 16 halvings cannot separate the stencil it raises an error instead of
returning a velocity. The requested probe floor is not a guaranteed actual
minimum after this check. This guard does not prove a converged
principal-value limit: coplanar overlaps, near-singular conditioning and
very short differences need independent study; it is not a complete wake
geometry validator.

On an 80% interpolation between the initial wake and the prior guarded
10x12 snapshot, with the Galerkin wall re-solved on that geometry, the
default unchecked probe has slip RMS 0.731958 and maximum 10.13539. The
guard adjusts 6 faces (at most 12 halvings), giving RMS 0.028646 and
maximum 0.189321. Requested probe fractions 0.0275 and 0.01375 give
guarded RMS 0.028344 and 0.028279. This is a direct regression for the
known jump-crossing mechanism, not an accuracy certificate; see
`cross_small_probe_guard_blend08_final.json`. The original full guarded
snapshot has a neighboring face extremely close to face 190's center and
is refused after 16 halvings, rather than yielding an unchecked velocity.

The 30x32, 10c sheet-update case was re-run with a 10-update budget:
all 11 sampled states reported zero adjusted probe faces, final CL was
0.2648144812, slip RMS 0.018976 and the preceding raw map residual
0.473416c. The final accepted backtracking factor was 5.96e-8. Status was
`iteration_limit`, because the budget ended before attempting the next
update; it is not a re-run of the subsequent `blocked_geometry` event.
The saved history is `../wake_grid_length/probe_guard_final_full_10c.json`.
The small 10x12 run likewise required no probe adjustments in 16 updates
and remained at `iteration_limit`, slip RMS 0.011937.

Reproduce with single-thread myml:


Local VSPAERO 3.55 logs show an outer `Wake Iter 1/5 ... 5/5`, with a GMRES
iteration inside each wake iteration; the existing
`docs/archive/issue/ISSUE49_WAKE_SENSITIVITY.md` also records a 1/3/5/10/20/40
`WakeIters` sensitivity sweep. This observation does not establish all
possible early termination conditions in VSPAERO. The configured GitHub

verified.

## Fixed Update And Time Comparison

Both current free-wake paths execute the requested number of updates;
meeting convergence tolerances does not end the loop. A failed geometry
guard still stops early, while successful completion reports `completed`
with an independent final `converged` flag. Iteration zero is the initial
fixed-wake solve, so 16 updates give states 0 through 16. The sheet
convergence assessment now evaluates its raw map on the final geometry.

Windows/myml, CPU, BLAS/OMP/MKL single-thread, rectangular NACA0012,
4 degrees, 10x12 wall, 8 wake segments, 10c initial wake length,
relaxation 0.25, order-3 Galerkin, pressure Kutta; filament core 0.05c
and two ribbons per span interval. The optional filament intersection
guard was off, as the sheet path has no equivalent guard. A final
nonadjacent-face audit found zero intersections in both snapshots; it
does not check shared-node pairs or wall crossings.

| 16 updates | Sheet update | Filament update |
|---|---:|---:|
| CL | 0.258200 | 0.258325 |
| Own-model slip RMS | 0.011937 | 0.012769 |
| Own map residual/c | 0.011193 | 0.076713 |
| Elapsed seconds, one run | 20.59 | 15.53 |

Neither converged; both used full relaxation at every update. The two raw
map residuals are different mappings and must not be interpreted as the
same error. The elapsed values are single runs including static assembly,
wall solves, wake velocity and callbacks, excluding the independent final
audit. The sheet study first runs a fixed-wake case in the same process;
the filament study starts fresh, so this is a timing screen, not a
repeated controlled benchmark. At about 15.5 seconds the recorded sheet
state was update 11 (15.29 s), the filament state update 16 (15.53 s).
The update-11 sheet snapshot was reproduced in a separate solve.

| ~15.5 s states | Sheet geometry, step 11 | Filament geometry, step 16 |
|---|---:|---:|
| P1 probe slip RMS | 0.012054 | 0.017230 |
| Ribbon slip RMS, core 0.05c / 2 | 0.020444 | 0.012769 |

Each geometry looks better under its own motion velocity model; the two
orders reverse under cross evaluation. Both geometries pass the same
limited final nonadjacent audit. These results do not identify an
independent physical truth, establish convergence, or generalize to the
30x32 geometry which previously hit the sheet orientation guard.
Raw histories are `fixed_time_sheet_10c.json` in

the cross evaluations are `equal_time_cross_sheet_11.json` and
`fixed_budget_cross_filament.json`. Commands and parameters are in each
